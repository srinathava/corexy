A deliberately ideal world

Assume a perfectly inextensible, taut belt and ideal pulleys — a bit of a spherical-cow model. Real carriages need linear guides. Here, only the explicitly enabled motions are allowed, so we can isolate the belt-length constraints.

About the model & real machines

The two clamps travel with the belt; they do not slide along it. With the motor locked, the material length from the motor to a clamp is fixed. Pulley wraps contribute constant lengths.

Rearranging is a setup operation: two routing idlers are added and the belt is rethreaded. Its settled loop length is the same before and after. Motion controls pause during this transition.

Coordinates use x rightward and y upward. A and B are signed belt displacements relative to center, not absolute motor angles. The mirrored belt is shown offset by 8 drawing units to distinguish the two belt planes.

Real CoreXY machines use guides and a rigid gantry. Belts keep moving mass low and let motors stay on the frame, but stretch, tooth deformation, tension, and bearing compliance affect stiffness. The diagonal-coordinate idea is a kinematic architecture, not a property unique to belts.
